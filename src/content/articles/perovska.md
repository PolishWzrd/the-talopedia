The Governorate-Generalship of Perovska (Russian: Генерал-Губернаторство Перовска, General-Gubernatorstvo Perovska; unofficial Cortesian: Gubernatorstwo Generalne Perowskiej) is a country situated in [[valtheria]], occupying an area of 1,657,722 km². It is bounded by [[rudania]] to the west, [[giathka]] to the south, and the Himalayan Ocean to the east. Its northern frontier follows the former colonial boundary. The country has an official population of 34,274,000, although the accuracy of this figure is uncertain owing to the absence of recent censuses in several frontier districts. The capital and largest city is Kyakhta, with a population of approximately 2,350,000.

Perovska was established as a Rudanian colonial administration in 1873. Following the conclusion of the Rudanian Civil War in 1920, the territory became the principal refuge of the defeated White movement, receiving large numbers of military personnel, their families, merchants, and civilian immigrants. The present administration is headed by a Governor-General and governed through the Perovsky General Staff. Although nominally an extension of the former imperial colonial administration, Perovska exercises de facto independence from Rudania and maintains its own military, currency, and administrative institutions.

## Etymology

The name Perovska derives from General Perovsky, a Rudanian military officer of [[cortesia|Cortesian]] ancestry responsible for establishing the original colonial administrative district in 1873. The name was subsequently extended to the entire territory by the present Governor-General.

The adoption of the historical designation was intended to preserve continuity with the former Rudanian imperial administration and reinforce the legitimacy of Perovska's claims to the institutions and territories of the former empire. Official communications frequently describe the present administration as the successor to the original colonial undertaking, a position which may facilitate future territorial and diplomatic claims against the People's Republic of Rudania.

## History

Perovska was established in 1873 as an administrative and military possession of the Rudanian Empire. Its original purpose was the exploration and exploitation of the region's natural resources, the development of commercial routes, and the extension of imperial authority into the surrounding steppe territories.

The early colonial administration was largely military in character. Numerous officers considered unsuitable for service in the imperial heartland or disgraced by the crown were assigned to the region, where the remoteness of the territory afforded them considerable independence from the central government. This circumstance contributed to the development of several intellectual and political movements, particularly among Cortesian-descended and Kalmyk settlers. The latter increasingly identified themselves with the Sarmat origin traditions of the Cortesian military nobility, regarding Perovska as a prospective new homeland rather than a place of exile.

The colonial administration also permitted the settlement of certain political prisoners, who were granted rights to acquire land and, in some instances, participate in local government by executive decree of the General-Governor. These policies, unusual among contemporary colonial administrations, contributed to the development of a relatively independent merchant and landed class.

During the Rudanian Civil War, a native revolt spread throughout the territory, motivated principally by ethnic and religious grievances. The various insurgent formations, frequently organized under local [kurbashis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorbaji), proved unable to establish a unified administration. Their activities were increasingly characterized by plundering and the seizure of property, and they were eventually defeated by the remaining colonial military authorities.

Following the defeat of the White armies in the Rudanian mainland, Perovska became an important refuge for the remnants of the imperial military establishment. The [principal withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Siberian_Ice_March) took place in 1920, bringing with it considerable numbers of officers, soldiers, their families, merchants, and civilian refugees. The desert frontier provided a natural obstacle against the advancing revolutionary forces, and an unofficial armistice subsequently developed between the opposing administrations.

The Governorate-Generalship was reorganized under Major General Dmitri Mikhailovich Skobelev, who assumed the office of Governor-General following an election among the remaining military electorate. The administration retained the colonial institutions while establishing an independent military command and currency. Since then, the General Staff has concentrated its efforts on the consolidation of the territory, the suppression of remaining insurgent movements, and the development of the country's economic and military infrastructure.

## Government

The Governorate-Generalship is governed by the All-Perovsky General Staff, which exercises supreme executive and military authority. The current Governor-General is Major General Dmitri Mikhailovich Skobelev, elected by an electorate which consisted principally of officers, senior non-commissioned officers and various influential administrators of the old colonial regime, who voted overwhelmingly in his favour.

The General Staff exercises direct authority over the armed forces and maintains considerable influence over civilian administration. A rudimentary legislative assembly and an independent judiciary exist for the administration of ordinary civilian affairs, although their jurisdiction is restricted in territories designated as being under direct military control.

The Transitory Council serves as an advisory institution and is intended to form the basis of a future civilian government, within it exists the Independent Department for Civil Affairs which acts as the precursor for the future judiciary. It exercises its authority principally in the larger urban settlements, however, certain judicial proceedings may be transferred to special military tribunals by order of the General Staff.

The country is divided into eight military districts plus the capital's Special District, which constitute its principal territorial administrative divisions:

The North-Western and North-Eastern Special Frontier Districts are nominally administered from Khod and Akshau respectively, although their actual headquarters are mobile and presently accompany the 1st and 2nd Ataman Corps.

In the southern districts, considerable authority remains in the hands of the native aristocracy, particularly the *beks* and former *noyons* of the displaced khanates and emirates. Their authority is formally recognized in certain territories, although government advisors stationed in the region exercise considerable influence over their affairs, particularly through the distribution of arms, ammunition, and other supplies.

Several native rulers have entered into agreements with the General Staff, retaining their estates and local privileges in exchange for the provision of auxiliary troops and the recognition of military authority. The administration regards these arrangements as an important instrument of pacification.

Official decrees provide for the eventual establishment of a mixed civilian and military government once the northern and southern frontiers have been sufficiently secured however no definite date for this transition has been announced.

## Military

The **Perovsky Group of Forces** constitutes the principal armed establishment of the Governorate-Generalship. It is organized into regular army formations, border guards, native auxiliary forces, Ataman formations, and tribal irregulars. The Supreme Command is situated in Kyakhta and falls under the authority of the Governor-General.

The regular army consists of 12 Rifle Divisions and 5 Cavalry Divisions, numbering approximately 124,300 men and possessing 984 guns. The Border Guard Command maintains 70 detachments, equivalent in strength to regiments, with approximately 84,000 men and 560 guns.

Native Auxiliary Forces comprise 17 regiments, numbering approximately 17,000 men. The irregular formations include the 1st Ataman Corps, consisting of two brigades, and the 2nd Ataman Corps, comprising one reinforced brigade, an independent Rifle Regiment, and an independent Artillery Regiment. Together, the two corps number approximately 13,450 men and possess 72 guns.

Tribal irregular militias constitute a further 12 regiments, numbering approximately 9,600 men. The total established military forces amount to approximately 290,350 personnel and 1,896 guns.

The administration maintains additional reserves, with a statutory peacetime establishment not exceeding 2% of the total population and a wartime mobilization capacity estimated at between 8 and 10%. The latter represents approximately 2.74 to 3.43 million persons.

The Perovsky Air Corps constitutes the country's air arm, while the Perovsky Coastal Flotilla is responsible for maritime defence. Both remain comparatively underdeveloped and are heavily influenced by Nichirin, which provides military advisors and maintains extensive basing rights in return for the protection of Perovskan maritime approaches against piracy and foreign violations. A total of 36 modern biplanes purchased from Nichirin constitute the basis of the Air Corps. The Perovsky Coastal Flotilla is comprised of 4 [auxiliary cruisers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_merchantman), ex-merchant vessels converted and armed for military service as well as 9 torpedo boats.

The military administration is hampered by the enormous area of the country and the inadequacy of its communications infrastructure. District commanders frequently exercise considerable operational independence, reporting to the Central General Staff and receiving new standing orders at intervals of approximately four to six months. Telegraphic communications with the northern Ataman formations are particularly unreliable.

The northern frontier is presently occupied by the 1st and 2nd Ataman Corps, whose commanders have conducted repeated punitive expeditions against suspected revolutionary remnants and nomadic bands. The General Staff has encountered increasing difficulties in maintaining control over these formations, whose operations frequently extend beyond their assigned areas.

In the south, military operations are directed against the continuing [Karakozli revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basmachi_movement), a recent result of which has been the impromptu re-mobilization of the so-called Gray Army, also known as the Thousand Banner Army by an independent movement of officers. The latter consists largely of loyalist native formations and seeks to establish order along the frontier, the lack of which is blamed on the local administration, with certain extremist elements advocating an advance into Giathka.

## Economy

The economy of Perovska is predominantly state-directed, although private property is legally protected and private commercial enterprise is permitted. The General Staff exercises considerable influence over production, transportation, and foreign trade, particularly in districts of military importance.

The original colonial economy was established primarily for the exploitation of natural resources and the development of commercial routes towards the Rudanian mainland. Agriculture developed principally along the river valleys and irrigated lands of the former khanates and emirates. The cultivation of these territories has expanded considerably since the arrival of White émigrés, who brought with them capital, agricultural experience, and technical expertise.

The indigenous population is particularly involved in herding and agriculture with many continuing to practice their traditional trades in furs, semi-precious materials, as well as providing labour for the developing mining industry. The administration has undertaken the construction of roads and railway connections to facilitate the transportation of these resources towards industrial and commercial centres.

The arrival of White-aligned merchants, landowners, and civilian refugees following the Civil War has considerably stimulated economic activity. Many of the immigrants brought substantial personal wealth, which has contributed to the development of urban commerce and the establishment of new enterprises.

Special Economic Zones have been established in several districts, where reduced taxation and relaxed commercial regulations are intended to encourage domestic and foreign investment. Foreign merchants are permitted to operate upon registration with the Foreign Affairs Department of the General Staff in Kyakhta and subsequent clearance from the relevant district headquarters.

The national currency is the Perovsky ruble, backed by reserves of silver and gold. The silver reserves are obtained principally through local extraction and transactions with the native population, while a considerable portion of the gold reserves was brought into the territory by the White armies during their withdrawal from Rudania.

Railway development is concentrated around Kyakhta, which serves as the principal administrative and commercial centre. Lines connect the capital with various productive and resource-bearing districts. A single principal railway connection extends eastward to the port settlement of Aktau, providing the country's principal access to maritime commerce.

Aktau, established shortly before the Great War, served as an important logistical centre for the White movement during the Civil War. Its harbour remains essential to Perovskan foreign trade. The absence of an adequate independent naval force has, however, permitted considerable smuggling and unauthorized maritime activity along the eastern coast.

## Geography

### Topography

Perovska occupies an area of 1,657,722 km² and is characterized by extensive steppes, desert regions, mountain ranges, and fertile river valleys. The northern and central portions consist principally of open steppe and semi-arid terrain, while the southern districts gradually give way to extensive desert formations.

The northern frontier follows the former colonial boundary and is characterized by elevated terrain and mountain passes. These regions have historically served as routes for nomadic migrations and military expeditions. Their remoteness and limited infrastructure have made the establishment of permanent administrative control difficult.

The western frontier is formed principally by Lake Balkhash, which separates Perovska from Rudania. Beyond the lake and the surrounding arid territories lies the Frontier Military District, where both states maintain extensive military establishments. The desert terrain has historically provided a considerable obstacle to large-scale military movements and has contributed to the present uneasy armistice.

The southern frontier with Giathka is characterized by mountainous terrain surrounding several important passes. These mountains form a natural defensive barrier, although the numerous routes through them have historically facilitated trade, migration, and armed incursions.

The eastern coastline extends along the Himalayan Ocean. The principal maritime settlement is Aktau, which serves as the country's principal port and commercial outlet. The coastline was until recently insufficiently patrolled, with the absence of a substantial navy having permitted the activities of smugglers and independent maritime traders.

The principal river system is the Kaskad-Gol, which originates in Primorye and traverses the interior of Perovska. Its valleys provide much of the country's most productive agricultural land and support numerous settlements. Lakes Gusino and Dusya flank the capital of Kyakhta from the west and east respectively, while Lake Primorye forms the source of the Kaskad-Gol.

The principal urban centres include Kyakhta, Kuryk, Ruzhev, Dzhalka, Khod, Ikan, Dubienka, Achikulak, Ulan-Khol, Zavkhan, Bayetovo, Moynaq, Kaplan, Akhsau, Vanino, Zaton, Kaseki, and Pribalkhashstroy. The latter, an important industrial settlement, was recently captured from Rudanian forces in 1933.

### Climate

The climate of Perovska is predominantly continental and semi-arid, with considerable variations resulting from differences in elevation and proximity to the sea.

The northern and central steppe regions experience hot summers and cold winters, with relatively little precipitation. The river valleys are considerably more fertile and have historically supported extensive agricultural settlements.

The southern districts are predominantly arid, with extensive desert regions and high summer temperatures. Precipitation is scarce, and the availability of permanent water sources is a principal determinant of settlement and agricultural development. In the South-West, sandstorms are frequent and constitute a considerable obstacle to farming and navigation.

The mountainous frontier regions experience lower temperatures and greater seasonal variations. Winters are particularly severe in elevated districts, where snow and difficult terrain frequently obstruct communications along underdeveloped routes.

The eastern coastal regions are subject to maritime influences, although the interior remains largely continental in character.

## Demographics

### Population

The official population of Perovska is estimated at 34,274,000. These figures are based partly upon family censuses conducted during the colonial period and subsequent administrative estimates. A comprehensive census has not been possible since the establishment of the present administration, particularly in the northern and southern frontier districts due to ongoing operations and civil unrest in these regions.

The urban population is estimated at 6,875,000, while approximately 27,399,000 inhabitants reside in rural settlements and agricultural communities. Kyakhta, with a population of approximately 2,350,000, is the largest city and principal centre of administration.

### Ethnic Groups

The population of Perovska is composed principally of Cortesian-descended settlers, Kalmyks, Rudanians, and native Karakozli. The combined Cortesian-Kalmyk-Rudanian population constitutes approximately 65–70% of the inhabitants in the central and northern districts, although its proportion decreases towards the southern frontier.

The Cortesian-descended population originally arrived as political exiles and military settlers during the colonial period. The relative isolation of the territory permitted the development of a distinctive cultural identity, influenced by the Sarmat origin traditions of the Cortesian nobility. These traditions became particularly prominent among the military officer class and the merchant population, many of whom came to regard themselves as a distinct Perovskan landed and intellectual elite.

The Kalmyks constitute another important element of the northern and central population. Their interaction with the Cortesian-descended settlers and Rudanian colonists contributed to the development of the characteristic cultural synthesis associated with the country.

Rudanians arrived throughout the colonial period as merchants, administrators, settlers, and political exiles. Their numbers increased considerably following the Civil War, when military personnel and civilian refugees accompanying the White armies settled throughout the territory.

The [Karakozli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmens) constitute the principal indigenous population throughout Perovska and especially of the southern districts. They are predominantly nomadic or semi-nomadic and retain traditional tribal institutions under the authority of local *beks* and other hereditary leaders. Their proportion of the population increases towards the Giathka frontier, where the combined Cortesian-Kalmyk-Rudanian population falls to approximately 40%.

### Languages

Rudanian is the official language of the Governorate-Generalship and is employed in the administration, military, and principal educational institutions.

Sarmacki, a dialect of Cortesian, is widely spoken among the Cortesian-descended and Kalmyk communities, particularly in the northern and central settlements. Karakozli is spoken throughout the southern districts and among the indigenous nomadic population.

### Religion

[Nazarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity) is the predominant religion among the Cortesian-descended, Kalmyk, and Rudanian populations, particularly [Sorovite Nazarism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy). It retains considerable influence within the military establishment and the principal urban settlements.

[Shahadism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam) is the principal religion of the Karakozli population, particularly in the southern districts. Religious institutions remain closely connected with the traditional tribal and aristocratic structures of the region.

A number of Karakozli have converted to Nazarism as part of the "civilizing mission" of the earlier colonial administration, with many having since entered the service of the administration. Such converts constitute an important element of the Honor Guard Corps, an elite formation responsible for the protection of the General Staff and its principal garrison in Kyakhta.

### Citizenship

White émigrés who settled in Perovska during or following the Civil War are recognized as full citizens. This includes former military personnel, their families, merchants, and civilian refugees who accompanied the withdrawal of the White armies in 1920. Individuals claiming Rudanian ancestry may apply for citizenship upon successfully completing an extensive security investigation, often requiring to be vowed for by an existing family member or otherwise "close associate" who has previously served or is serving in the Perovsky armed forces.

Applicants may also be admitted by service to Perovska by either demonstrating verifiable proof of previous anti-communist activity in the form of white resistance in occupied Rudania or by serving for no less than 3 consecutive years in one of the many volunteer regiments of the Southern and South-Western military districts.

The administration maintains strict procedures concerning naturalization owing to the possibility of revolutionary infiltration and espionage by agents of the People's Republic of Rudania.
